Privacy notice

Last updated October 19, 2026

KaVerse is a small app that shows you the Bible verse your closest friends are reading. This notice explains, plainly, what we collect, why, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. It is written to meet the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

Who is responsible. Rei Cordero, an individual developer based in the Philippines, is the personal information controller. Contact: the KaVerse support group on Telegram.

What we collect

When you sign in. You sign in with Apple or Google; we never see a password. We receive an account identifier, your email address (with Apple this may be a private relay address), and your name. From Google we may also receive a link to your profile photo; we store the link, not the photo.

Your profile. The name you confirm, your photo link if any, your app language, when you joined, and when you last opened the app (used only to show you which verses are new).

What you write. Verses you share, stored as a reference (book, chapter, verse, translation) and never as the verse text, with your optional note (up to 80 characters). Prayer requests (up to 180 characters) and their expiry time if you set one. Who can see each post: everyone in your circle, your Core, or a group you made. Your reactions.

Your circle. Who you have added or asked to add, who you marked as Core, people you blocked, and reports you filed (the reason and any note). Invite links you create, and which invite brought you in, if any.

Usage events. A short list of events, for example "a verse was attached", "a prayer request was posted" or "a translation was read", with no text, no verse, no prayer content and no name. Each event is tied to a scrambled code made from your account ID with a secret key, not to your account ID itself. These help us decide which translations and features to keep.

Abuse limits. A per-hour count of certain actions, for example opening invite links, so the app can slow down misuse.

On your phone only. Your sign-in session, recent posts for fast loading, and the snapshot the home-screen widget shows. These stay on your device and are cleared when you sign out or delete your account.

What we do not collect. No location, no contacts, no phone number, no advertising identifier, and no tracking across other apps or websites. There is no third-party analytics or advertising SDK in the app.

Why we use it

To run the service you asked for: showing your posts to the people you chose, and theirs to you. To keep people safe: blocks, reports and abuse limits. To decide what to build, using the scrambled usage events only. Prayer requests often mention health or family matters, sometimes about other people, so we treat them as the most sensitive data we hold: they never appear on the widget, they can expire, and their content never enters usage events.

Who sees it

  • Your circle sees what you post, limited to the audience you chose. An expired prayer request is visible only to you. People you blocked see nothing of yours.
  • Only you see who reacted "prayed" to your prayer request.
  • The operator can read reported posts in order to review them, and can access the database to run the service.
  • Service providers process data for us: Supabase (database, sign-in and server functions; servers in Singapore), and Apple and Google (sign-in). When you read a translation we do not host ourselves, our server asks API.Bible for the verse text using only the verse reference. No information about you is sent.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything you wrote: until you delete your account. Then it is deleted immediately.
  • Prayer requests: if you set an expiry, the request stops being shared at that time and stays visible only to you, until you delete your account.
  • Usage events: about 90 days. After that the individual events are deleted and only daily totals remain, with no code linked to anyone.
  • Abuse-limit counts: overwritten every hour.
  • Backups: our database provider keeps backups for a limited period it sets; deleted data disappears from them as they expire.

Your rights and choices

Under the Data Privacy Act you may be informed, access, object, correct, delete, get a copy of your data, and complain.

  • Get a copy. In the app, open You, then Your data, then Export my verses. You get a file of everything you wrote, including your own prayer requests. It does not include anything other people wrote, or the scrambled usage events.
  • Delete your account. Open You, then Your data, then Delete my account. It happens immediately and cannot be undone. Everything you wrote is removed from your friends' feeds and widgets, your threads and reactions go, your usage events are deleted, and if you signed in with Apple we tell Apple to end KaVerse's access to your Apple ID. Two things remain: what other people wrote, and any copy a friend already saved outside the app. A friend's phone may show your last verse until their app next refreshes.
  • Correct your name in the app. For anything else, contact us.
  • Complain to the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph) if you think we got this wrong.

Children and young people

KaVerse is intended for people aged 13 and over. It is used by church circles, which include young people. There are no ads and no public profiles: posts reach only people the user added, in person or by an invite link.

Security

Data is sent encrypted (HTTPS). Visibility is enforced by the database itself, not only by the app. The key behind the usage codes is held on the server.

Changes

If this notice changes in a way that matters, we will say so in the app before it takes effect.
